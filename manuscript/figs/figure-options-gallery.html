Figure concept gallery

Six visual directions for the manuscript: three ways to explain its position (Figure 1) and three ways to make the proposed evaluation design concrete (Figure 7). These are editable SVG mockups embedded in one HTML page, not final artwork.

Reading the mockups: bars and outcome marks are intentionally absent or labeled as qualitative. They should not imply experimental results. Figure 1 should carry the paper’s thesis; Figure 7 should show the comparison a researcher would run.

Figure 1 · Position summary

Pick the story the opening figure should tell
Option 1 · Recommended

From aligned agents to governed outcomes

A causal chain: why individual alignment does not settle group behavior, and what institutional design adds.

Best for: a clear thesis and story arc. Tradeoff: less detail on the taxonomy.
Option 2 · Same scenario, three rules

One commons, three governance conditions

Keep the current “same agents” setup, but compare conditions with qualitative consequences instead of schematic bars.

Best for: a memorable visual example. Tradeoff: risks implying the third design already works.
Option 3 · Systems view

Nested agent, group, and institution

Show the unit of analysis as nested layers, with levers and human accountability around the group.

Best for: defining “model-in-institution.” Tradeoff: more conceptual, less narrative.

Figure 7 · Evaluation design

Pick how to turn the position into a research program
Option 1 · Recommended

Factorial evaluation blueprint

Make the variables, repeated runs, and outcome families explicit. Highlight interaction tests, not a “winning” cell.

Best for: operational clarity and reviewer response. Tradeoff: denser than a simple matrix.
Option 2 · Contrast matrix

Show the comparisons, not made-up scores

Retain the cross of agent group × institution, but leave cells unscored and highlight planned contrasts.

Best for: a clean visual anchor. Tradeoff: protocol details need caption or nearby text.
Option 3 · Research workflow

From benchmark to corrective action

Make the evaluation agenda usable as a sequence, including the feedback loop from failure to redesign.

Best for: practitioner guidance. Tradeoff: the factorial comparison is less prominent.
