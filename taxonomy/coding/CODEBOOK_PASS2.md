# Pass-2 codebook (64 mechanisms, 18 themes)


## T01 Visibility & attribution

- **M01 Conduct monitoring & transparency**: Agents' actions are observed, inspected, audited, or disclosed so deviations can be detected. _Examples: arms control verification; inspections; BRA-Audit; collusion audit pipeline; digital contact tracing; forest patrols_
- **M02 Communication & internal-state oversight**: A monitor inspects or constrains the messages or internal states agents exchange, to detect or prevent covert coordination. _Examples: activation-probe collusion monitor; communication monitoring; multi-agent interpretability probes; paraphrasing / passive oversight; Verifiable Latent Alignments_
- **M03 Tamper-evident records**: Actions are recorded in an attributable log that cannot be altered undetectably and can be audited. _Examples: audit log; Certificate Transparency; PeerReview accountability; receiver-attested receipts (Sello)_
- **M04 Identity & attribution**: Agents carry persistent, verifiable identities (and costly new ones) so actions and consequences attach to a responsible party. _Examples: agent IDs, real-time monitoring, activity logs; agent infrastructure (attribution); Agent Name Service; Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off; DMARC policy; dues-paying newcomers / once-in-a-lifetime identifiers_

## T02 Reputation

- **M05 Reputation records & scores**: A persistent record or score of past conduct is kept and made available to others. _Examples: Certificate Transparency logs; COOPER; eBay feedback forum; eBay feedback profiles; feedback redesign; Global Reputation_
- **M06 Peer evaluation & review**: Peers assess an agent's conduct or output and publish or aggregate the assessment. _Examples: Birdwatch / bridging algorithm; Community Notes; distributed moderation / meta-moderation; OECD peer review; Universal Periodic Review_
- **M07 Gossip**: Agents pass information about third parties' conduct to others who may interact with them. _Examples: gossip; gossip (ALIGN); prosocial gossip; RepuNet reputation via gossip_

## T03 Exclusion & conditional access

- **M08 Expulsion**: A deviator is removed from the group, resource, or platform. _Examples: community ban; deplatforming; exile / membership removal; expulsion by majority vote; ostracism; ostracism / exile_
- **M09 Temporary suspension**: A deviator is excluded or cut off for a bounded period or until a condition is met. _Examples: beam tagging; circuit breaker; interaction limits; licence suspension; tag-out beam_
- **M10 Partner choice & tie severing**: Agents choose, keep, or drop interaction partners based on their observed or reported conduct. _Examples: biological market; EigenTrust; FIRE trust model; G-Safeguard edge pruning; image scoring / eavesdropping; network rewiring_
- **M11 Shared blocklists & revocation**: A shared list of flagged agents or revoked credentials lets many parties refuse the same deviators. _Examples: certificate revocation list; denylist; DNS blacklists (DNSBL)_
- **M12 Access & terms conditioned on record**: Access, privileges, or prices depend on an agent's reputation or proof of compliance. _Examples: CARP reputation penalty; CONFIDANT; reputation score / credit system; risk-rated premium / deductible; school immunization requirements; sender guidelines / spam-rate threshold_
- **M13 Membership boundaries**: Insiders defend a resource against outsiders or impose costs on non-members to induce joining. _Examples: climate club tariffs; harbor gangs / territoriality_

## T04 Cost imposition

- **M14 Peer punishment**: A peer pays a cost to reduce a deviator's payoff. _Examples: altruistic punishment; community sanctions for desertion; costly norm punishment; costly sanctions; direct punishment; explicit punishment_
- **M15 Third-party punishment**: An unaffected observer sanctions a deviator. _Examples: third-party punishment_
- **M16 Centralized (pool) sanctioning & fines**: A central authority, often funded by members, applies fines or penalties for detected deviations under explicit rules. _Examples: automatic fine; enforcement school; fine for late pick-up; governance graph; governance graph (Institutional AI); HOPE probation_
- **M17 Externality pricing & central incentive adjustment**: A charge proportional to imposed harm, or taxes and subsidies set (often learned) by a planner, realign individual payoffs. _Examples: adaptive mechanism design; adaptive mechanism design planner; AI Economist tax planner; Falkinger tax-subsidy mechanism; learned Pigovian tax; Pigouvian tax_
- **M18 Shaming & social disapproval**: Deviations are met with non-material costs: disapproval, public exposure, or reputational pressure. _Examples: 90+30 disclosure policy; disapproval points; naming and shaming; peer pressure; shaming; vulnerability disclosure_
- **M19 Liability for harm**: The deviator bears the damages its action causes, or a guarantee is enforced when a claim proves false. _Examples: expected-damages payoff regulator; strict liability / negligence; warrant enforcement_
- **M20 Collective liability**: Group members share liability for any member's deviation, giving them reason to monitor one another. _Examples: collective punishment; cross-default / solidarity lending; group lending joint liability; joint liability; social collateral_

## T05 Rewards & payoff alignment

- **M21 Peer reward & gifting**: Agents transfer part of their own payoff to peers, typically cooperators. _Examples: gifting; gifting action; learned incentive function (LIO); peer reward; peer reward / gifting_
- **M22 Payment for cooperation**: A central party pays for a cooperative act, verified behavior, contribution, or outcome. _Examples: block reward; cash incentive for vaccination; payments for ecosystem services; piece-rate pay; Pigouvian subsidy; principal-agent contracts_
- **M23 Status & prestige**: Contributors receive visible status markers or prestige. _Examples: badges; prestige_
- **M25 Outcome sharing & aligned stakes**: Agents share in joint output or hold stakes in the system or in others' payoffs, so their payoff rises with collective success. _Examples: firm-wide on-time bonus; reward shares; reward transfer; Shapley incentive distribution; Shapley-Coop reward redistribution; skin in the game (PoS)_
- **M26 Relative-performance incentives**: Rewards depend on an agent's rank relative to others. _Examples: rank-order tournament; team tournaments / rank incentives_

## T06 Calibrated response

- **M27 Graduated & proportional sanctions**: Responses escalate with repetition or duration, or scale with the severity of the deviation. _Examples: adaptive punishment; adaptive punishment (APC); blocking policy; enforcement ladder (Contributor Covenant); exponential back-off; governance graph state machine_

## T07 Commitment & contracting

- **M28 Deposits, bonds & stakes**: An agent (or a third party on its behalf) posts collateral that is forfeited on breach or refunded on compliance. _Examples: breach remedy transaction; commitment savings contract (CARES); deposit contract; deposit-refund; environmental assurance bond; insurer agents_
- **M29 Binding contracts**: Agents agree to enforceable conditional transfers or penalties, often executed automatically. _Examples: contracts; formal contracts; formal contracts (MOCA); leveled commitment contract; decommitment penalty; liquidated damages_
- **M30 Self-commitment devices**: An agent binds its own future action or makes backing out costly. _Examples: commitment devices; commitment savings / gym contract; Markov Commitment Games; tying hands / sinking costs; voluntary commitment_
- **M31 Escrow & verify-then-pay**: Payment is held by a neutral mechanism and released only when performance is verified. _Examples: atomic service channels; blockchain A2A payments (x402); HTTP 402 settlement; blockchain escrow; RAILS clearing; Verify-then-Pay_
- **M32 Assurance contracts**: Contributions are refunded (possibly with a bonus) if a collective threshold is not reached. _Examples: dominant assurance contract; provision point with money-back guarantee_
- **M33 Pre-play communication & pledges**: Agents exchange non-binding messages or public pledges before acting. _Examples: chat room / face-to-face communication; cheap talk; cheap talk / communication; cheap talk / public pledge; cheap-talk channel; communication in CPR experiment_

## T08 Structural constraints

- **M34 Ex ante action restriction & scoped permissions**: Only pre-specified actions, scopes, zones, or time-bounded rights are permitted. _Examples: A2A Agent Card; authentication/authorization; authenticated delegation (OAuth/OIDC extension); BlockA2A; DIDs; smart-contract access control_
- **M35 Runtime interception & interruption**: An enforcement layer or overseer blocks, resamples, or halts an agent's actions as they happen. _Examples: Governance-as-a-Service / Trust Factor; kill switch; resampling protocols (Ctrl-Z); stop / injunction_
- **M36 Usage caps & quotas**: Hard limits bound each agent's use of a shared resource or its rate of action. _Examples: 429 Too Many Requests / rate limit; catch shares / ITQs; fair queueing; per-customer quotas / adaptive throttling; progressive risk vesting; rate limit_
- **M37 Rotation & turn-taking**: Access to shares of a resource is assigned in turns or by lot. _Examples: constrained MAPPO channel access; rotational lottery of fishing spots; water temple irrigation scheduling_
- **M38 Interaction structure**: Who interacts with whom, how often, and in what roles is designed: network topology, repetition, population composition. _Examples: communication-topology restriction; coordination protocols (star/chain/tree/graph); heterogeneity; network closure / social capital; network topology; network topology / personality placement_

## T09 Conditional strategies

- **M39 Direct reciprocity**: An agent conditions its behavior toward a partner on that partner's past behavior toward it. _Examples: amTFT; host sanctions; learned reciprocity; LLM-generated IPD strategies; partner fidelity feedback / partner choice; reciprocal altruism_
- **M40 Indirect reciprocity**: Agents help others according to the recipient's conduct toward third parties. _Examples: community enforcement / contagious punishment; donor game; donor game with observed behaviour; donor observes recipient's behaviour; image scoring; indirect reciprocity_
- **M41 Group-conditional cooperation**: An agent adjusts its own contribution or use to what others do collectively or to aggregate signals. _Examples: self-enforcing international environmental agreement; slow start; AIMD; TCP congestion control (RFC 5681)_

## T10 Collective choice & legitimacy

- **M42 Collective choice of rules**: Members propose, vote on, or veto the rules or allocations that govern them. _Examples: agent-authored executable rules; agent-authored governance rules; Apache voting; lazy consensus; veto; consensus participatory budgeting_
- **M43 Robust aggregation & quorum**: Decisions require agreement among independent parties and discount faulty or outlying inputs. _Examples: Byzantine agreement / majority vote; Byzantine-robust evaluator ranking; confidence-weighted voting among oracles; CP-WBFT; PBFT; resilient consensus filter_
- **M44 Legitimate & elected authority**: Authority to lead, monitor, or sanction is assigned by election or recognized as legitimate. _Examples: elected leadership; elected monitor; fixed/elected/rotating manager; normative module; project leader / constitution_
- **M45 Institutional choice & exit**: Agents choose which institutional regime to join, or may opt out. _Examples: endogenous sanctioning organization; institutional self-selection; sanctioning vs sanction-free institution; sanctioning vs sanction-free institution choice; SanctSim institution choice; voluntary participation_
- **M46 Nested & adaptive rule-making**: Rule-making is layered or delegated, and rules are revised or redesigned over time. _Examples: adaptive governance; constitutional rules; evolved constitution; rational design of institutions; WikiProject governance_

## T11 Disclosure incentives

- **M47 Leniency for self-reporting**: Agents that disclose their own or joint wrongdoing receive reduced penalties. _Examples: corporate leniency / amnesty; leniency; leniency program; leniency; whistleblowing; truth and reconciliation amnesty_
- **M48 Rewards for reporting others**: Agents are paid for reporting others' violations or flaws, and reporting is made cheap. _Examples: bug bounty; qui tam / whistleblowing; security.txt; whistleblower rewards / leniency_

## T12 Checks on enforcement

- **M49 Meta-norms**: Agents who fail to sanction deviators are themselves sanctioned. _Examples: metanorm; metanorms_
- **M50 Checks on sanctioners**: Safeguards limit the abuse of sanctioning power (filters on antisocial punishment, audits of officials, collective checks on dominance). _Examples: antisocial punishment filter; audit office / courts / charter; counter-punishment; reverse dominance hierarchy_

## T13 Dispute resolution

- **M51 Third-party adjudication**: A designated or peer adjudicator issues a binding ruling, possibly after tiered escalation. _Examples: Arbitration Committee; conformal social choice; debate; decentralized adjudication; judicial module; Kleros decentralized arbitration_
- **M52 Mediation**: A neutral third party helps parties reach agreement without imposing a ruling. _Examples: AgentMediation; coordinated vulnerability disclosure coordinator; mediation; structured mediation_
- **M53 Bonded challenge**: Agents stake funds to challenge a claimed outcome, winning if upheld and losing if not. _Examples: Arbitrum challenge protocol; permissionless fault proofs; token-curated registry_
- **M54 Appeals & error correction**: Sanctioned agents can contest decisions, and wrongful outcomes are reversed. _Examples: appeals / contestability; appeals of automated moderation; false-positive correction; hard fork; Oversight Board_

## T14 Repair & restoration

- **M55 Restitution & reintegration**: The offender repairs harm or apologizes, and the relationship is rebuilt or membership restored. _Examples: apology / confession; gacaca / community reconciliation; rehabilitation; restitution / repair_
- **M56 Undoing deviation's effects**: The output of a deviation is reverted, removed, or rolled back so deviation yields nothing. _Examples: AgentGit commit/revert; moderator comment removal; reverts / algorithmic rejection tools; saga transactions; worker policing; worker policing / coercion_
- **M57 Failure attribution & incident analysis**: After a failure, evidence is used to identify responsible agents and causes. _Examples: automated failure attribution; BFT forensic support; incident analysis framework_

## T15 Norm formation & internalization

- **M58 Explicit norms & codes of conduct**: Expected behavior is written down, published, and explained, including when sanctions are applied. _Examples: Best Current Practice (RFC 2914); code of conduct; Contributor Covenant; TAB; conflict mediator; managerial model_
- **M59 Instilled prosocial dispositions**: Agents' preferences or reasoning are shaped (by training or prompting) to value cooperative outcomes. _Examples: Chain of Social Thought; evolved intrinsic motivation; intrinsic moral rewards; universalization prompt; universalization prompting_
- **M60 Defaults & choice architecture**: The cooperative option is made the default. _Examples: auto-enrolment nudge_
- **M61 Norm learning**: Agents infer norms, or which institution is authoritative, from observed sanctions and behavior. _Examples: Bayesian rule induction; CRSEC; public sanctioning events; public sanctions_

## T16 Social learning

- **M62 Imitation & strategy selection**: Higher-payoff strategies spread through copying or selection. _Examples: algorithm tournaments; cultural evolution; evolutionary IPD tournament; evolutionary simulation; Fermi imitation; Fermi imitation rule_

## T17 Mutual aid

- **M63 Mutual aid, pooling & mutual credit**: Members pool resources, give without direct exchange, or trade services in mutual credit. _Examples: bail fund / solidarity group; credit-based forwarding (Sprite); gift economy; mesh network; ROSCA; ROSCA (random/bidding)_

## T18 Mechanism design & allocation

- **M64 Incentive-compatible mechanisms**: Allocation and payment rules make truthful reporting or cooperation each agent's best strategy. _Examples: AgentLance; VCG-style payment; Augur REP reporting; Groves-Ledyard mechanism; redesigned public-goods reward; stable matching clearinghouse_
- **M65 Market & matching allocation**: Tasks or resources are allocated through auctions, markets, or central matching rather than free negotiation. _Examples: Agent Exchange (AEX); deferred acceptance / TTC matching; sandbox economy auctions; skill-based outsourcing_
